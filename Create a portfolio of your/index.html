Oil

Oil is a nonpolar chemical substance primarily composed of hydrocarbons, which is hydrophobic (does not mix with water) and lipophilic (mixes with other oils). It can refer to various types, including petroleum, which is a depletable, non-renewable resource used for energy and is a significant contributor to air pollution and climate change. Oils are typically liquid at room temperature and can be found in various forms, such as essential oils, mineral oils, and cooking oils. In the context of energy, crude oil is a major fossil fuel extracted from the ground, contributing to about one-third of the world's primary energy.

petrol

Petroleum, also known as crude oil or simply oil, is a naturally occurring, yellowish-black liquid chemical mixture found in geological formations, consisting mainly of hydrocarbons.[1] The term petroleum refers both to naturally occurring unprocessed crude oil, as well as to petroleum products that consist of refined crude oil.

CNG

CNG is natural gas that has been compressed to less than 1% of its volume at standard atmospheric pressure. It primarily consists of 90-95% methane (CH4), with small amounts of other hydrocarbons such as ethane, propane, and butane. CNG is stored in high-pressure cylinders, typically at pressures of 3,000 to 3,600 psi.
